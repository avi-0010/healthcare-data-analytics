Executive Summary — 6 Key Business Insights
📊
Patient volume is evenly distributed across all six conditions
Arthritis leads at 9,218 patients; Asthma is last at 9,095 — a spread of only 123 patients across 6 conditions. This indicates consistent network-wide intake rather than specialty concentration.
💰
Obesity is the highest-cost condition at $25,859 average billing
Obesity outbills Cancer (lowest, $25,207) by $652 per patient. Across 9,146 Obesity patients annually, this differential represents a meaningful revenue and clinical resource concentration.
📈
Billing has been flat at ~$25,500 for five years
Monthly average billing has held between $24,900 and $26,400 across the full 2019–2024 period. With rising operational costs, static pricing may be compressing margins over time.
🔬
Patient age has zero predictive power for billing cost
The correlation between age and billing amount is -0.003 — effectively zero. Condition type and treatment pathway, not patient demographics, are the primary cost drivers.
⚠️
6,038 emergency patients had abnormal test results
These cases represent emergency admissions where diagnostic tests returned abnormal values — a significant patient safety flag requiring clinical quality review, protocol audit, and potential follow-up care coordination.
🏦
Payer mix is near-perfectly balanced across 5 insurers
Cigna (20.3%) through Aetna (19.7%) are within 0.6% of each other. This eliminates single-payer revenue concentration risk and strengthens negotiating leverage with any individual insurer.
Management Takeaways
⚠ Immediate Action
Review 6,038 Emergency + Abnormal Cases
Emergency admissions with abnormal test results must be reviewed by clinical quality teams. Each case is a potential adverse event, liability exposure, or missed follow-up care opportunity. A structured audit protocol is recommended.
💼 Revenue Strategy
Five Years of Flat Billing Warrants Pricing Review
Average billing has not meaningfully increased since 2019. As staff costs, supply chains, and inflation rise, unchanged billing structures likely compress net margin. A cost-of-care–aligned billing review is overdue.
📅 Capacity Planning
July Is the Peak Emergency Month — Plan Accordingly
Emergency admissions peak in July (1,573) and trough in February (1,452) and November (1,462). Seasonal staffing models, supply orders, and bed allocation should reflect this 8.3% swing in demand across the calendar year.
🏥 Clinical Investment
Obesity Prevention Programs Could Reduce High-Cost Volume
With 9,146 Obesity patients generating the highest average billing ($25,859), targeted preventive care and wellness programs addressing Obesity could reduce future high-cost admissions while improving long-term population health outcomes.
🔒 Risk Monitoring
~5,500 High-Cost Patients Require Finance Review
Approximately 5,497 patients (top 10% by billing, above ~$45,000) warrant case-level review for accurate coding, cost allocation, and care appropriateness. Systematic review of these cases often surfaces both savings and revenue recovery opportunities.
📌 Network Strength
Balanced Payer Mix Supports Contract Renegotiation Leverage
All five insurance providers hold an almost identical ~20% share of patient volume. No single payer dominates, which means the hospital has equalized leverage for future contract negotiations and is protected against one-payer volume concentration.
