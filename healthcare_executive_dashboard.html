Executive summary

The findings most relevant to management attention

01 / PATIENT MIX

Condition counts are tightly grouped

Arthritis has the highest record count (9,218), only two records above Diabetes. No single condition dominates the six-condition mix.

02 / BILLING

Obesity has the highest average billing

Average billing for Obesity is 25,858.56, followed by Diabetes at 25,713.98. Cancer has the lowest average among the six conditions at 25,206.63.

03 / AGE

Age is not a useful linear billing signal

The age–billing correlation is −0.003, indicating virtually no linear relationship in this dataset.

04 / COVERAGE

Cigna is the most represented insurer

Cigna appears in 11,139 records (20.3% of records); this is record representation, not a verified unique-member count.

05 / OPERATIONS

Emergency cases need a review pathway

6,038 records are both Emergency admissions and marked Abnormal on test results. These records may warrant clinical review prioritization.

06 / COST MONITORING

Monthly average billing fluctuates

The monthly values shown range around 24.9k–26.3k in the selected visible months. The notebook output does not expose every monthly value, so a full trend line is not reconstructed here.

Management takeaways

Where leadership attention can be most useful

01

Maintain balanced condition-level readiness

Condition record counts are similar across all six categories. Plan capacity using demand, staffing and clinical service data rather than the top-ranked condition alone.

02

Investigate billing drivers, not age alone

Age has near-zero linear correlation with billing. Review length of stay, admission type, condition and other available operational factors before drawing cost conclusions.

03

Prioritize exception review

Route emergency records with abnormal results for appropriate clinical review, and separately examine the high-billing cohort for explainable drivers and data quality.

04

Improve coverage reporting

The source has no unique patient identifier. Treat insurer counts as record counts and avoid interpreting them as unique covered lives until a reliable identifier is available.
